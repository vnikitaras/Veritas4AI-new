LEGAL REFERENCE (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 "Digital Omnibus on AI")

A) Scope (Art. 2): research-only systems developed and put into service solely for scientific research (Art. 2(6)) and research/testing/development before placing on the market or putting into service (Art. 2(8), except real-world testing) are outside the Regulation until they are placed on the market or put into service. A source such as an academic paper or open-source repository does not by itself show an intended purpose in a regulated context.

B) Art. 5 prohibited practices (screen each; mark only if the documentation indicates it): subliminal/manipulative or deceptive techniques causing significant harm; exploiting vulnerabilities (age, disability, socio-economic situation); social scoring leading to detrimental treatment; predicting criminal offences solely from profiling/personality traits; untargeted scraping of facial images to build facial-recognition databases; emotion inference in the workplace or education (except medical/safety reasons); biometric categorisation to infer sensitive attributes (race, political opinions, trade-union membership, religious beliefs, sex life, sexual orientation); real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement (narrow exceptions).

C) Art. 6(1) + Annex I (product route): the AI system is a safety component of a product, or is itself a product, covered by Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I (for example Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on medical devices, Regulation (EU) 2017/746 on in-vitro diagnostic devices, Directive 2006/42/EC machinery, Directive 2009/48/EC toys, Regulation (EU) 2019/2144 motor vehicles, radio equipment, lifts, pressure equipment, etc.) AND that product is required to undergo a third-party conformity assessment under that legislation. Both conditions are required. Application date under the Omnibus: 2 August 2028.

D) Art. 6(2) + Annex III (stand-alone route). Application date under the Omnibus: 2 December 2027. The areas and points are:
 1. Biometrics (insofar as use is permitted by Union or national law): (a) remote biometric identification systems - EXCLUDING biometric verification whose sole purpose is to confirm that a specific natural person is who they claim to be (1:1 verification/authentication); (b) biometric categorisation according to sensitive or protected attributes; (c) emotion recognition.
 2. Critical infrastructure: safety components in the management and operation of critical digital infrastructure, road traffic, or the supply of water, gas, heating or electricity.
 3. Education and vocational training: (a) determining access/admission/assignment; (b) evaluating learning outcomes; (c) assessing the appropriate level of education an individual will receive or access; (d) monitoring/detecting prohibited behaviour of students during tests.
 4. Employment, workers management and access to self-employment: (a) recruitment or selection (placing targeted job advertisements, analysing and filtering applications, evaluating candidates); (b) decisions on terms of work relationships, promotion or termination, allocating tasks based on individual behaviour or personal traits, monitoring and evaluating performance and behaviour of workers. NOTE: a tool used by job seekers for their own CV preparation is not by itself a system used by an employer or recruiter for selection.
 5. Access to and enjoyment of essential private services and essential public services and benefits: (a) evaluating eligibility of natural persons for essential public assistance benefits and services (including healthcare), and granting, reducing, revoking or reclaiming them; (b) evaluating creditworthiness or establishing a credit score of natural persons (EXCEPT systems used to detect financial fraud); (c) risk assessment and pricing for natural persons in life and health insurance; (d) evaluating and classifying emergency calls, dispatching or prioritising emergency first-response services, emergency healthcare patient triage. NOTE: medical diagnostic tools, clinical software and general healthcare AI are NOT point 5(a) (it covers eligibility for public assistance benefits and services such as social security, welfare, public housing, including the healthcare services granted as such a benefit); they are assessed under Annex I (medical devices) instead. A general information or citizen-service chatbot is not in point 5(a) unless it evaluates eligibility for, or decides on granting/reducing/revoking, essential public benefits or services.
 6. Law enforcement (risk assessment of victimhood or offending, polygraphs, evidence reliability, profiling in investigations, etc.).
 7. Migration, asylum and border control (polygraphs, risk assessment, examination of applications, detection/recognition of persons).
 8. Administration of justice and democratic processes (assisting judicial authorities in researching/interpreting facts and law; influencing election or referendum outcomes).

E) Art. 6(3) derogation: an Annex III system is NOT high-risk if it does not pose a significant risk of harm to health, safety or fundamental rights, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision-making, AND it meets at least one of: (a) performs a narrow procedural task; (b) improves the result of a previously completed human activity; (c) detects decision-making patterns or deviations without replacing or influencing the previous human assessment without proper human review; (d) performs a preparatory task for an assessment relevant to the Annex III use cases. EXCEPTION: an Annex III system that performs profiling of natural persons is ALWAYS high-risk. A provider relying on Art. 6(3) must document the assessment (Art. 6(4)).

F) Art. 50 transparency obligations (can apply IN ADDITION to any class): (1) systems intended to interact directly with natural persons must make clear they are interacting with an AI system; (2) providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text must mark outputs in a machine-readable format; (3) deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation systems must inform exposed persons; (4) deployers of deepfakes, and of AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, must disclose.
