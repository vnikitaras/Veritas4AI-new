DETAILED FIELD REQUIREMENTS:

1) APP_DESCRIPTION
Type: long text (string).
Length: 700–1,000 words.

Content requirements:
- Provide a precise, technically rich description of the AI system.
- Describe, in structured paragraphs (still within a single text value):
  - The main purpose and core functionalities of the system, focusing on AI-driven components (e.g. recommendation, ranking, classification, detection, scoring).
  - The key data sources and data flows (what types of data, from which actors, how frequently, and for which processing purposes).
  - The high-level architecture at conceptual level (e.g. data ingestion, feature processing, model training, inference, feedback loops, monitoring).
  - The typical lifecycle stages (design, data collection, training, validation, deployment, monitoring, decommissioning) as they apply to this application.
- You MUST explicitly explain how AI outputs influence real-world decisions or user experience (e.g. what is displayed, promoted, suppressed, flagged).
- You MUST include one paragraph titled in the selected language as “Limitations and information gaps”, listing what the documentation does not state about:
  - Scale (number of users or interactions).
  - Data volume.
  - Complexity of the model landscape (e.g. number and type of AI models).
- The description should be sufficiently detailed to support Annex IV technical documentation: a regulator reading only APP_DESCRIPTION should understand what system is being assessed and why it may be high-risk or impactful.

2) APP_BUSINESS_CONTEXT
Type: long text (string).
Length: 500–800 words.

Content requirements:
- Explain how the AI system is embedded into the broader business model and operational context of the organisation.
- Cover:
  - The main business objectives that the system supports (e.g. engagement, content curation, fraud detection, safety, operational efficiency).
  - How the system contributes to revenue generation, cost optimisation or regulatory compliance.
  - The organisational units and functions that depend on this system (e.g. product teams, policy teams, operations, compliance, risk).
  - How decisions produced or influenced by the AI system can have direct or indirect impact on users, customers, third parties and public interest.
- Explicitly link this business context to EU AI Act concerns:
  - For example, how the system may touch upon fundamental rights, access to services, informational autonomy, or other protected interests.
- Include at least one paragraph that states which aspects (maturity of the organisation, scale of operations, role of this AI-driven functionality in the business) are not documented, and acknowledges the resulting uncertainty.

Type: long text (string).
Length: 400–700 words.

Content requirements:
- Provide a structured narrative assessing how critical this AI system is from:
  - A business continuity perspective.

- Explicitly relate criticality to EU AI Act concepts:
  - Possible inclusion in Annex III categories.
  - Proximity to Article 5 prohibited practices, if any.
  - The need for strong risk management (Article 9), data governance (Article 10), transparency (Article 13), human oversight (Article 14) and robustness/cybersecurity (Article 15).
- Provide a qualitative classification (e.g. low, medium, high criticality) and justify it with concrete arguments.
- Include at least one paragraph stating which undocumented facts (e.g. user base, role of the system in decision chains) would change this criticality assessment.
